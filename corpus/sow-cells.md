## Lesson 1: Parts of a cell and their functions

Week 1 · Outcomes: IS8.2.1.a
Lesson focus: Name the main cell parts and state what each does.
Learning experiences: Teacher uses an analogy of the school as a cell: the gate as the cell membrane, the head teacher's office as the nucleus, the compound as cytoplasm. Learners study labelled charts of plant and animal cells and complete a matching activity linking each part to its function.
Resources: Charts or projected micrographs of plant and animal cells, matching cards.
Assessment: Matching activity; oral questioning.

## Lesson 2: Building cell models

Week 1 · Outcomes: IS8.2.1.b
Lesson focus: Represent plant and animal cells with models and compare them.
Learning experiences: Groups build an animal cell model using a clear polythene bag filled with thick porridge (cytoplasm) and a large seed (nucleus), and a plant cell model by placing a similar bag inside a small box (cell wall) with green beans (chloroplasts) and a water-filled balloon (vacuole). Groups compare the two models and complete a similarities and differences table.
Resources: Clear polythene bags, porridge or gelatine, seeds, beans, small boxes, balloons.
Assessment: Model presentation rubric; comparison table.

## Lesson 3: Diffusion in liquids and gases

Week 2 · Outcomes: IS8.2.2.a
Lesson focus: Observe and explain diffusion.
Learning experiences: Learners place a drop of food colour in a beaker of still water and observe it spread over ten minutes without stirring. The teacher sprays a little perfume at the front of the class and learners raise a hand when they smell it. Learners explain diffusion using the terms high concentration, low concentration and concentration gradient, and give examples in living things such as oxygen entering blood in the lungs.
Resources: Beakers or clear bottles, food colour, perfume or air freshener.
Assessment: Learners draw and label before and after diagrams.

## Lesson 4: Osmosis with potato strips

Week 3 · Outcomes: IS8.2.2.b
Lesson focus: Investigate how water moves into and out of plant cells.
Learning experiences: Groups cut potato strips of equal length, measure them, place one set in plain water and one in strong salt solution, and wait thirty minutes. While waiting, the teacher explains a partially permeable membrane using a sieve analogy. Learners re-measure the strips, feel their firmness, record results and explain the changes.
Resources: Potatoes or arrow roots, knives for teacher use, rulers, salt, water, containers, timer.
Assessment: Results table and written explanation.
Safety: Only the teacher or supervised learners use knives.
