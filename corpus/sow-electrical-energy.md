## Lesson 1: Series circuits

Week 1 · Outcomes: IS8.3.1.a
Lesson focus: Set up and draw a series circuit.
Learning experiences: Teacher reviews circuit symbols for a cell, bulb, switch and wire. Groups connect two bulbs one after the other with a dry cell and switch, close the switch and observe. Learners draw the circuit diagram with standard symbols and describe the single path for current.
Resources: Dry cells with holders, torch bulbs with holders, switches, connecting wires, circuit symbol chart.
Assessment: Correct circuit set-up and labelled diagram.
Safety: Use only dry cells; never connect to mains sockets.

## Lesson 2: Parallel circuits

Week 1 · Outcomes: IS8.3.1.a
Lesson focus: Set up and draw a parallel circuit.
Learning experiences: Groups connect two bulbs on separate branches across the same dry cell. They trace each path with a finger, draw the circuit diagram and compare the number of paths with the series circuit from Lesson 1.
Resources: Same kit as Lesson 1.
Assessment: Circuit diagram and short comparison sentence.

## Lesson 3: Comparing brightness

Week 2 · Outcomes: IS8.3.1.b
Lesson focus: Explain differences in bulb brightness and the effect of a broken bulb.
Learning experiences: Groups set up series and parallel circuits side by side with identical bulbs and compare brightness. They unscrew one bulb in each circuit and record what happens to the other bulb. Class discussion explains why houses and classrooms are wired in parallel.
Resources: Two complete circuit kits per group.
Assessment: Observation table and explanation of house wiring.

## Lesson 4: Electrical safety at home and school

Week 2 · Outcomes: IS8.3.1.c
Lesson focus: Identify electrical hazards and respond safely.
Learning experiences: Learners examine pictures showing overloaded sockets, damaged cables, wet hands near switches and illegal connections, list the hazard in each and propose a safe action. Groups role-play responding to someone receiving an electric shock: switch off at the mains, do not touch the person directly, use a dry wooden object, call for help.
Resources: Hazard picture cards, role-play scenario cards.
Assessment: Hazard identification worksheet; role-play observation.
