## [IS8.1.1.a] Classify substances as elements or compounds based on their composition

Sub-strand: 1.1 Elements and Compounds
Key inquiry question: How can we tell whether a substance is made of one kind of atom or more than one?
Suggested learning experiences: Learners sort samples and picture cards of common substances (copper wire, charcoal, water, table salt, sugar, aluminium foil) into elements and compounds and justify each choice. Learners discuss why a compound cannot be separated by physical means.
Core competencies: Critical thinking and problem solving; Communication and collaboration.
Values: Unity when working in groups; Responsibility when handling samples.

## [IS8.1.1.b] Write chemical symbols of the first twenty elements of the periodic table

Sub-strand: 1.1 Elements and Compounds
Key inquiry question: Why do scientists use symbols instead of full names for elements?
Suggested learning experiences: Learners study a periodic table chart, identify the first twenty elements, practise writing symbols with correct capitalisation (Na, Cl, Ca, Mg), and play a symbol matching game in pairs.
Core competencies: Learning to learn; Digital literacy where a periodic table app is available.
Values: Respect for scientific conventions.

## [IS8.1.2.a] Prepare simple acid-base indicators from locally available plant materials

Sub-strand: 1.2 Acids, Bases and Indicators
Key inquiry question: How can plants help us find out whether a substance is acidic or basic?
Suggested learning experiences: Learners crush coloured plant materials such as red cabbage, beetroot, hibiscus or bougainvillea flowers, extract the colour using warm water or ethanol, filter the extract and store it in labelled containers.
Core competencies: Creativity and imagination; Self-efficacy.
Values: Responsibility in the safe use of materials; Patriotism through using local resources.
Pertinent and contemporary issues: Environmental conservation when collecting plant materials.

## [IS8.1.2.b] Classify household substances as acidic, basic or neutral using indicators

Sub-strand: 1.2 Acids, Bases and Indicators
Key inquiry question: Which substances in our homes are acids and which are bases?
Suggested learning experiences: Learners test lemon juice, vinegar, baking soda solution, soap solution, wood ash solution, salt solution and water using plant extract indicators and litmus paper, record colour changes in a table and classify each substance.
Core competencies: Critical thinking and problem solving; Communication and collaboration.
Values: Integrity in recording observations honestly.

## [IS8.1.2.c] Describe uses of acids and bases in everyday life

Sub-strand: 1.2 Acids, Bases and Indicators
Key inquiry question: How do acids and bases make our daily lives better?
Suggested learning experiences: Learners discuss uses such as antacids for indigestion, lime for treating acidic soils, vinegar in food preservation and soap for cleaning, then create a poster linking each use to a property of acids or bases.
Core competencies: Citizenship; Communication and collaboration.
Values: Social justice in sharing knowledge with the community.
Pertinent and contemporary issues: Health education on the safe use of antacids.
