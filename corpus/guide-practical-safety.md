## Handling chemicals and indicators

Outcomes: IS8.1.2.a, IS8.1.2.b, IS8.2.2.a
Keep all chemicals in labelled containers. Learners never taste or smell substances directly; demonstrate wafting instead. Use the smallest volumes that show a clear result. Ethanol is flammable and must be kept away from open flames. Wood ash and soda solutions are caustic, so learners should rinse any splashes with plenty of clean water immediately. Plan a hand-washing point before the lesson.

## Electrical practicals

Outcomes: IS8.3.1.a, IS8.3.1.b, IS8.3.1.c
Use only dry cells for learner circuits. Check wires for exposed copper before the lesson. Learners must never connect circuit kits to mains sockets. If wires become hot, open the switch and disconnect the cell. Keep water and drinks away from the practical area.

## Improvised and low-cost materials

Outcomes: IS8.1.1.a, IS8.2.1.b, IS8.2.2.b, IS8.3.1.a
Where a school lacks laboratory equipment, use clear plastic bottles cut in half instead of beakers, cloth instead of filter paper, and torch bulbs with wire twisted around them instead of bulb holders. Cell models can be built from polythene bags, porridge and seeds. Ask learners to bring clean empty containers a week ahead. Improvised materials should still be labelled and stored safely.

## Managing large classes during practicals

Outcomes: IS8.1.2.b, IS8.2.2.b, IS8.3.1.a
Assign group roles: materials manager, recorder, timekeeper and reporter, rotating each lesson. With classes above forty learners, run a teacher demonstration first and then stations where groups rotate every ten minutes. Give each group a printed step card so instructions do not depend on hearing the teacher across the room.
