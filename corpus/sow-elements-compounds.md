## Lesson 1: Elements and compounds around us

Week 1 · Outcomes: IS8.1.1.a
Lesson focus: Distinguish elements from compounds using everyday substances.
Learning experiences: Display real samples (copper wire, charcoal, aluminium foil, table salt, sugar, water). Learners discuss in groups what each substance is made of. Introduce an element as a substance made of one kind of atom and a compound as two or more elements chemically combined. Groups sort picture cards into two columns and present one justification each.
Resources: Real samples in labelled containers, picture cards, chart paper, marker pens.
Assessment: Oral questions; observation of card sort accuracy.
Reflection prompt: Which substances did learners find hardest to classify and why?

## Lesson 2: Why compounds cannot be separated physically

Week 1 · Outcomes: IS8.1.1.a
Lesson focus: Contrast mixtures and compounds by how they can be separated.
Learning experiences: Teacher demonstrates separating a mixture of sand and iron filings using a magnet. Learners predict whether iron sulphide or table salt can be separated the same way, then discuss why a chemical bond needs a chemical change to break. Learners complete a comparison table of mixtures and compounds.
Resources: Magnet, sand, iron filings, sealed sample of iron sulphide or picture, comparison table worksheet.
Assessment: Completed comparison table; exit question.

## Lesson 3: Chemical symbols of the first twenty elements

Week 2 · Outcomes: IS8.1.1.b
Lesson focus: Read and write chemical symbols with correct capitalisation.
Learning experiences: Learners locate the first twenty elements on a periodic table chart. Teacher explains that symbols start with a capital letter and any second letter is lower case, with examples where symbols come from Latin names (Na for sodium, K for potassium). Pairs play a symbol matching game, then write symbols from dictation.
Resources: Periodic table chart, element name and symbol cards, exercise books.
Assessment: Symbol dictation of ten elements; peer marking.

## Lesson 4: Elements and compounds in daily life

Week 2 · Outcomes: IS8.1.1.a, IS8.1.1.b
Lesson focus: Apply classification and symbols to real products.
Learning experiences: Learners read labels of household products (salt, toothpaste, baking powder) and identify elements and compounds named on them, writing symbols for any of the first twenty elements they find. Groups present one product to the class.
Resources: Clean empty product packages, periodic table chart.
Assessment: Group presentation checklist.
